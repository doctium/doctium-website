# How an AI Symptom Checker in Nigeria Works: Meet Leenah

When something feels wrong, the first question most people ask is simple: is this serious, or can it wait? An AI symptom checker in Nigeria is built to help you answer exactly that, without guessing on your own. Leenah, the symptom checker inside Doctium's telemedicine app, listens to what you are feeling, asks follow-up questions, and helps you decide what to do next. It does not diagnose you. It does symptom triage, then points you to the right kind of care, whether that means booking a verified doctor or heading straight to urgent care.

This matters because health worries rarely arrive at convenient times. You might be awake at 2am with a feverish child, or unsure whether a headache and a stiff neck are nothing or something. Leenah is there to bring calm structure to that moment.

## What Leenah actually is

Leenah is a safety-first AI assistant that helps you describe and sort your symptoms. Think of it as a careful first conversation, not a verdict. You tell Leenah what is happening in your own words. It asks clarifying questions, the way a triage nurse might: how long, how severe, what makes it better or worse, what else you are noticing.

From there, Leenah helps you understand how urgent your situation looks and what your next step should be. It is the front door to care inside the Doctium app, connecting you to MDCN identity and licence-verified doctors when you need a real consultation.

## Safety first: red-flag detection that outranks the model

The most important thing to understand about Leenah is how it handles danger signs. Leenah uses red-flag detection that outranks the AI model itself. In plain terms, certain symptoms trigger fixed safety rules that the model cannot talk around or soften.

If you describe something that could signal an emergency, such as chest pain, severe difficulty breathing, signs of stroke, heavy bleeding, or a serious reaction, Leenah does not try to reason its way to a gentler answer. The rule wins. You are told clearly and quickly to seek urgent, in-person care. This design choice exists so that the technology stays on the side of caution every single time, not just most of the time.

## Symptom triage in five Nigerian languages, by voice or text

Health is personal, and people explain their bodies most clearly in the language they think in. Leenah supports symptom triage in five languages: English, Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo. This multilingual approach means a trader in Kano, a student in Enugu, and a parent in Lagos can each describe symptoms naturally and be understood.

Leenah also accepts voice input and can reply with voice. If typing is hard, or you simply prefer to speak, you can talk to Leenah and listen to its responses. That makes the tool genuinely usable for people who are unwell, in a hurry, or more comfortable speaking than reading.

## What Leenah is not

Being clear about limits is part of being safe. Leenah is not a diagnosis. It does not name a disease and it does not prescribe medication. It cannot replace a physical examination, laboratory tests, or the judgement of a licensed clinician.

Instead, Leenah does the useful work of organising your symptoms and routing you well:

- It helps you decide whether to book a doctor or seek urgent care now.
- It hands you off to a verified doctor for an actual consultation through secure chat, voice, or encrypted video.
- It supports the doctor, who remains the one making every clinical decision.

A telemedicine app is also not the right place for an emergency. If you or someone with you is in immediate danger, do not wait on a chat. Get to the nearest emergency facility.

No. Leenah is an AI symptom checker that helps with triage and routing. It does not diagnose illness or prescribe medicine. A licence-verified doctor makes every clinical decision, and you can connect to one inside the app.

### What languages does Leenah understand?
Leenah works in five languages: English, Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba, and Igbo. You can use text or voice, and Leenah can reply with voice as well.

### What happens if I describe an emergency?
Leenah's red-flag detection outranks the AI model. If your symptoms suggest an emergency, you are clearly directed to seek urgent, in-person care. Telemedicine is not for emergencies; go to the nearest emergency facility right away.
