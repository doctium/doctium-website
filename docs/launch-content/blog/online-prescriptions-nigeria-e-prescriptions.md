# Online Prescriptions in Nigeria: How Verified E-Prescriptions Work

Getting medicine in Nigeria too often starts at the pharmacy counter instead of with a clinician. You describe a symptom, someone hands you a drug, and you hope it was the right call. Online prescriptions in Nigeria can change that order of events. With Doctium, you speak to a verified doctor first, and if a medicine is appropriate, you receive a signed digital prescription you can take to a pharmacy with confidence. This guide explains how an e-prescription is issued, how it is verified, and where the line sits between what is safe to prescribe online and what is not.

## What an online prescription actually is

An online prescription is a prescription written by a licensed doctor after a remote consultation, instead of a paper slip handed to you in a clinic. On Doctium, that consultation happens over secure chat, voice, or encrypted video with a doctor whose identity and licence are verified with the Medical and Dental Council of Nigeria (MDCN). The doctor takes a history, asks questions, and makes a clinical judgement. Only then, if medication is the right step, do they issue the prescription.

The important part is the order. The medicine follows the assessment. A digital prescription is not a shortcut around seeing a clinician; it is the record of a clinician deciding that a specific drug, at a specific dose, is appropriate for you.

## How a Doctium e-prescription is issued and verified

Once your consultation is complete and your doctor decides to prescribe, the e-prescription is created inside the platform and **cryptographically signed**. That signature ties the prescription to the verified doctor who wrote it, so it cannot be quietly altered or faked after the fact.

Each prescription carries a **QR code**. A pharmacist can scan that code to confirm the prescription is genuine, that it came from a real verified doctor, and that the details have not been tampered with. This matters in a market where falsified prescriptions and substandard medicines are a known problem. Verification turns "trust me" into "check it yourself."

From there, the prescription moves through a **pharmacy hand-off**, so you are not left holding a screenshot and hoping a pharmacy will honour it. The path from consultation to medicine stays inside one traceable flow:

- A verified MDCN doctor assesses you over secure chat, voice, or video.
- If medication is appropriate, a signed e-prescription is generated.
- The prescription carries a QR code the pharmacy can scan to verify it.
- The prescription is handed off to a pharmacy for dispensing.

## What is safe to prescribe online, and what is not

Telemedicine is well suited to many situations: prescription refills for stable chronic conditions, follow-ups, common infections a doctor can confidently assess by history and visible signs, and routine medications where your history is clear. Chronic-care programs on Doctium can track vitals and goals over time, which makes ongoing prescriptions safer because the doctor sees a fuller picture.

Some things genuinely need to be seen and touched in person. A remote doctor may decline to prescribe and refer you for a physical visit when the situation calls for examination, tests, or imaging. Controlled substances and high-risk medications are handled with particular caution. A responsible refusal to prescribe online is a sign the system is working, not failing you.

And to be clear: telemedicine is not for emergencies. Chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, stroke symptoms, or a collapsing patient need in-person emergency care now, not a chat window. If symptoms are severe or worsening, go to the nearest hospital.

## Why this beats buying medicine over the counter

Self-medication is convenient until it is not. The wrong antibiotic feeds resistance. A masked symptom delays a real diagnosis. A dose that is fine for one person harms another. An online prescription puts a licensed decision-maker between you and the medicine, while still respecting your time. You get the speed of buying at a pharmacy with the safety of a doctor's judgement and a verifiable record of what was prescribed and why.

It also leaves you with documentation. A signed digital prescription is a clean record you, your doctor, and your pharmacist can all rely on, which is far better than a faded paper slip or a verbal instruction you half remember.

## FAQ

**Are online prescriptions legal and legitimate in Nigeria?**
Prescriptions issued through Doctium come from doctors whose identity and licence are verified with the MDCN, after a real consultation. Each e-prescription is cryptographically signed and QR-verifiable, so a pharmacy can confirm it is genuine.

**Can I get any medicine I want through an online consultation?**
No. The doctor decides what, if anything, to prescribe based on your assessment. They may refer you for an in-person visit or tests, and high-risk or controlled medications are handled with extra caution. You cannot self-prescribe.

**How does the pharmacy know my e-prescription is real?**
The pharmacist scans the QR code on your prescription. That confirms it came from a verified doctor and has not been altered, before the medicine is dispensed.
