# Doctium Launches an AI-Native Hospital Operating System for African Healthcare

Doctium today announced the launch of its **AI-native hospital operating system** for African healthcare, a platform built to run the full clinical, financial and operational life of a hospital while keeping licensed clinicians in control of every decision. The launch spans two connected products: a hospital operating system with an AI-native EHR at its core, and a telemedicine marketplace of identity-verified doctors. Doctium is Nigeria-first by design, treating NHIA, HMO and insurance workflows as first-class rather than afterthoughts.

## A different thesis: from records to closed loops

Most hospital software is built to store records. Doctium is built so that every clinical, financial and operational workflow produces structured artifacts that feed intelligent closed loops, improving care quality, lightening documentation, reducing revenue leakage, speeding up operations and laying the groundwork for personalized medicine.

This is not a traditional EHR with a chatbot bolted on. Across the system, AI assists, recommends, summarizes, routes and monitors, but it never acts alone. Licensed humans make every final clinical decision. Rules outrank the model, and there is no autonomous prescribing. The framing is consistent across every module: doctor-in-the-loop.

## What is available at launch

**The Hospital OS** ships with an AI-native EHR and 14+ modules covering the real workflow of a hospital: Billing and Revenue; Insurance, HMO and NHIA; Inpatient and Wards; Pharmacy; Laboratory; Imaging; Nursing; Surgery; Emergency; Patient Administration; the Clinical Station and OPD; Health Information Management; Legal and Forms; and Blood Bank. The platform is FHIR R4 interoperable, so hospitals can exchange data with the wider health ecosystem.

**Doctium Scribe**, ambient clinical documentation, is built directly into the EHR. With patient consent, it captures the encounter through chat, dictation or a consented recording and turns it into a structured draft note covering history, examination findings, assessment, plan, referrals, orders, billing cues and patient instructions. The clinician reviews, edits and approves the note before anything enters the record. Scribe is consent-gated and recording access is logged. The result is lighter documentation burden, more complete charts, stronger billing and insurance documentation, and cleaner data downstream.

**Personalized medicine**, beginning with sickle cell disease, reflects the African clinical reality. Nigeria is the global epicenter of sickle cell disease, genotype testing is cheap and normalized, and hydroxyurea titration is effectively an n-of-1 problem. Doctium offers genotype-aware thresholds (SS, SC, AS), an explainable risk engine that names and weights its factors including live weather and harmattan conditions, a crisis diary, hydroxyurea titration with CBC safety flags, a daily monitoring agent and a pre-visit brief for the clinician. Hold-dose guidance goes to the doctor; patients never self-dose. Safety rails are never personalized, rules outrank the model, and the doctor stays in the loop.

**The telemedicine marketplace** connects patients to MDCN identity and licence-verified doctors. It is fronted by Leenah, a safety-first AI symptom checker and triage assistant in which red-flag detection outranks the model. Leenah works in five languages, English, Nigerian Pidgin, Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo, with voice input and voice replies. Consultations run over secure chat, voice and encrypted video. Prescriptions are cryptographically signed with QR verification and pharmacy hand-off, and chronic-care programs cover enrolment, vitals, threshold alerts and goals. The marketplace also includes wallets with Paystack and MediGram health-education videos. Both patient apps are available on iOS and Android.

## Built for the African context

Nigeria-first means the hard parts of local healthcare are designed in, not patched on: insurance and NHIA handling as a core module, multilingual triage that meets patients in the language they speak, and a risk engine that accounts for environmental factors that genuinely affect Nigerian patients. The same discipline runs through the platform. Sensitive actions are consent-gated and logged, and safety logic is kept separate from anything the model can influence.

## Responsible-use note

Doctium's AI features are designed to support clinicians, not replace them. Telemedicine is not for emergencies. Anyone experiencing a medical emergency should seek in-person or emergency care immediately. Final clinical decisions, including any prescribing, always rest with a licensed clinician.

## FAQ

**What is an AI-native hospital operating system?**
It is healthcare software where AI is part of the core workflow rather than an add-on. Every clinical, financial and operational task produces structured data that feeds closed loops for quality, documentation, revenue and operations, while licensed clinicians make every final decision.

**Is Doctium a replacement for our existing EHR?**
Doctium provides an AI-native EHR at the center of a wider hospital operating system with 14+ modules. It is FHIR R4 interoperable, so it can exchange data with other systems in your environment.

**Does the AI prescribe or make decisions on its own?**
No. AI assists, recommends, summarizes, routes and monitors. Rules outrank the model, there is no autonomous prescribing, and a licensed clinician is always in the loop.
